Editorial standards · Updated July 22, 2026

Evidence first, limitations included.

Our standards for sourcing, interpreting, reviewing, updating, and correcting educational content.

Scope and purpose

Young Leonidas publishes general education about peptide biology, veterinary research, evidence quality, and regulatory context. It does not diagnose an animal, establish a veterinarian-client-patient relationship, or provide treatment, dosing, purchasing, or sourcing instructions.

Evidence hierarchy

Articles distinguish molecular mechanisms, cell and tissue experiments, animal models, target-species studies, veterinary clinical evidence, and regulatory review. Evidence from one level is not presented as though it proves a claim at another level.

Source selection

Priority is given to regulatory agencies, peer-reviewed research, veterinary manuals, universities, and recognized professional organizations. Commercial claims and secondary summaries may identify a topic but are not treated as sufficient support for safety or effectiveness.

Authorship and expert review

Articles currently use the organizational byline “Young Leonidas Editorial.” That byline does not imply veterinary credentials. A specialist will be identified as an author or reviewer only when the person has actually contributed, their relevant qualifications can be stated accurately, and they have agreed to attribution.

Updates and corrections

Material revisions receive an updated date in the page and structured data. Corrections should change the inaccurate passage, preserve the article’s original purpose where possible, and add an explanatory note when the correction materially changes the conclusion. Routine style or formatting changes do not require a new modified date.

Independence and future commerce

The current publication does not sell peptide products. If products, sponsorships, affiliate relationships, or other commercial interests are introduced, they must be disclosed clearly and kept from determining editorial conclusions.

Clinical safetyReaders should discuss an individual animal’s symptoms, diagnosis, medications, and treatment decisions with a licensed veterinarian who knows that animal.
Primary standardMatch claims to evidence

A mechanism is not a clinical outcome, and another species is not the target species.

TransparencyNo invented authority

Credentials and review roles appear only when they are real and verifiable.
