## Publishing order

1. BPC-157 for dogs: evidence, safety, and missing clinical data.
2. Peptides for dogs: a broad pillar linking established and investigational topics.
3. TB-500 for dogs: thymosin-beta-4 research versus the marketed fragment.
4. Peptides for cats: species-specific metabolism and the thinner evidence base.
5. BPC-157 versus TB-500: evidence comparison without dosing or product advice.
6. Veterinary peptide glossary: stable definitions that can attract reference links.

## Quality gate

Every medical or veterinary topic must:

- distinguish mechanism, preclinical research, target-species research, clinical
  evidence, and regulatory review;
- cite primary or authoritative sources for material claims;
- avoid dosing, sourcing, treatment protocols, and product endorsements;
- state the actual evidence gap rather than using “research use” language as a
  substitute for safety;
- display author identity, publication date, modified date, and review status
  accurately.

## Measurement checkpoint

After at least 28 days of Search Console data, map each query to its ranking URL.
Prioritize impressions in positions 5–20, then identify cannibalization, low CTR,
and pages that need consolidation. Add third-party volume and difficulty data only
when its source and collection date can be recorded.
